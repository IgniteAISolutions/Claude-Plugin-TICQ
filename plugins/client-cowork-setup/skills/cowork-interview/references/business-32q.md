# The business interview

Once per organisation, 90 to 120 minutes. Run it before the build day, never on it.

Carried from the field manual this plugin was built from,
which is where the wording was field-tested. The questions are the manual's, unchanged.

This block is a human interview. There is nothing to paste into Claude while it runs.

## The running order, and why it is this order

Each block is scored by how hard it is on the client. The inventory is the expensive one
and it sits where their energy still reaches it.

| Block | Roughly | What it covers | Load |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm-up | 10 min | The firm, the people, how a day runs | Low, gets them talking |
| Org and roles | 10 min | Who does what, who will use the system | Low |
| The job flow | 15 min | One real job from enquiry to delivery | Medium, they know this cold |
| The task inventory | 35 to 45 min | Every recurring task, with time and frequency | High, this is the cognitive load |
| Tools, systems, data | 15 min | What they run on, what is sensitive | Medium, a deliberate dip after the inventory |
| Decision rights | 10 min | What they will let go of, what needs a human yes | Medium, sets up the firewall |

Take a real break every 40 minutes. The natural points are after the job flow and again
after the inventory. Take them. A tired client gives shallow answers, and shallow
answers build a shallow system.

## The client-effort truth

Three standing rules for whoever runs this.

Never apologise for the difficulty. Never rush to get to the building. Never describe
the client's part as light.

If you find yourself softening the load to spare the client, stop. You are about to
build them a thin system they will quietly abandon.

## Block 1, warm-up, 10 minutes

```
1. Tell me what the firm does, in the words you would use with a customer, not the
   words on the website.
2. How long has it been going, and how did it start?
3. Walk me through a normal day for you. Who do you talk to, what lands on your desk,
   what eats your time?
4. What does the firm do better than anyone you compete with?
5. What is the thing you keep meaning to fix and never get to?
```

## Block 2, org and roles, 10 minutes

```
6. Who is in the firm? Names, roles, what each one is responsible for.
7. Of those people, who will use the system day to day? Who is the one who has to want
   this for it to work?
8. Who makes the call when something is truly a judgement, not a process?
9. Who holds the knowledge that would walk out the door if they left tomorrow?
10. Is anyone here going to resist this? Who, and why?
```

## Block 3, the job flow, 15 minutes

```
11. Take me through one real job from the first enquiry to getting paid. What happens,
    in order, and who does each step?
12. Where does that job usually stall or go wrong?
13. Which steps are the same every single time, and which change job to job?
14. Where in that flow are you copying, pasting, retyping, or rebuilding something you
    have built before?
15. If you could hand one step of that flow to someone else tomorrow and never think
    about it again, which step?
```

## Block 4, the task inventory, 35 to 45 minutes

Build the table live with the client. Every recurring task gets a row.

```
16. Let us list everything you or your team do that repeats. I will write, you talk.
    Start with the things you do every week without fail.
17. (For each task as it comes up) How long does that take you, a typical time, start
    to finish? And how many times in a week?
18. What else repeats monthly rather than weekly? (Convert to a weekly average: a
    2-hour monthly task is roughly 30 minutes a week.)
19. What do you do that you dread, or put off, because it is dull and repetitive?
20. What does your team ask you the same questions about, over and over?
21. (For each row, classify) If a system did this and you just checked it, would that
    be safe? Or does this have to stay with a person?
```

### The table

| Task | Who runs it | Mins per instance | Instances per week | Mins per week | Classify |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

The classify column has three values and they are not interchangeable.

- **Agent-run.** A machine does it and a person checks the output. The system can finish
  and file it.
- **Owner-reviewed.** A machine drafts, a person decides before it goes anywhere. The
  draft stops at the gate.
- **Human-only.** Stays with a person. A sensitive conversation, a pricing judgement, a
  hiring call.

The mins-per-week column is the frozen measurement baseline. At day 30 you walk the same
list and re-ask the same two columns. Sum the before minus after per row, divide by 60,
and that is hours saved per week. The guarantee asks for a minimum of five.

Get it signed the same day. Read the table back, confirm each number is theirs, and take
initials on the printed table or a one-line email. An unsigned baseline is not a
baseline, because at day 30 the numbers become negotiable.

## Block 5, tools, systems and data, 15 minutes

```
22. What software does the firm run on? Walk me through the tools, what each one is for.
23. Where does your data actually live? The customer records, the financials, the
    documents?
24. Which of those systems could a build connect to, and which are locked down or
    off-limits?
25. What data in this business is confidential or regulated? Customer data, financials,
    anything with personal information in it, anything under a regulator's rules?
26. Is there anything that absolutely must not leave the building, or must not go near
    an AI tool, for legal or contractual reasons?
27. Who controls the logins and the permissions for those systems today?
```

## Block 6, decision rights, 10 minutes

```
28. Think about the work we have listed. What would you be comfortable a machine
    finishes and sends without you seeing it first? Be honest, the answer might be very
    little, and that is fine.
29. What must always cross your desk before it goes out, no matter how good the draft is?
30. Where money moves, what is the rule? Does anything financial ever happen without you
    saying yes?
31. Where something gets published or sent to a customer, what is the rule there?
32. If you imagine the system running for a month, what would make you trust it more?
    What would make you switch it off?
```

Blocks 5 and 6 write the firewall. Use the client's words rather than paraphrasing them,
because a rule in their own language survives and a rule in ours does not.

## The people note

**The over-holding pattern.** Most owners call everything human-only at first. Coach
them through it row by row. Do not argue. Ask what the worst outcome is if a machine
drafts it and they check it before it goes anywhere. Usually the honest answer is
"nothing, I would just want to see it first", which puts it in owner-reviewed.

Do not force it. A genuine human-only task stays human-only and you respect that. The
skill is telling the two apart, and the question above usually does it.

If you let them call everything human-only, you have built them nothing and rebuilt
their bottleneck inside Claude.

**The fading client.** The inventory is the most demanding stretch. When they fade, stop
and take the break. Do not push through to finish the list.

## Scripts for the hard moments

Field-tested. Say them as written.

**They are fading and the inventory is not done.**
> "Let us take 5 minutes. This is the hardest part of the whole day and you are doing
> the work that makes the system worth having. Grab a coffee, we will pick up the list
> when you are back."

**"Can you just figure this out, you are the expert."**
> "I could guess, but a guess builds you a system that runs on my assumptions about your
> business, not the truth of it. The reason this is hard is the reason it works. Ten
> more minutes on this and the rest of the day is building, which is the easy bit for
> you. Stay with me."

**They want to mark everything human-only.**
> "Tell me the worst that happens if a machine drafts this and you check it before it
> goes anywhere."

**They undersell a task's time to seem efficient.**
> "Be honest with me, not impressive. If it takes you an hour, tell me an hour. The
> bigger the real number, the bigger the saving I can show you in 30 days. Underselling
> it now just hides the win later."

**They want to add a shiny thing that is not on the task list.**
> "Good, let us log it. Is it something you actually do at volume now, or something that
> would be nice? If you do it weekly, it earns a row. If it is a someday, I will park it
> so we keep today focused on the work that is real."

**They apologise for not knowing an answer.**
> "No need. Half of this is stuff people rarely write down, that is exactly why we are
> doing it. Let us come back to that one, tell me about X instead."

## Coverage check before you close

Confirm you are leaving with all of it: answers to all 32 questions in their own words,
a signed task inventory with a real time against every row, who will use the system,
which data is off-limits, and the three gate rules for sending, money and publishing.

A gap here becomes a gap in the build, and the build day is the wrong place to find it.
