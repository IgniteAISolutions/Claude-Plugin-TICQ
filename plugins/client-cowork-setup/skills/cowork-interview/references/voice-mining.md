# Voice by mining

Taste is what somebody rejects. Eighty per cent of a useful voice profile is what the
person is not. That is what samples show and self-report hides.

## Step 1, get the samples

Ten pieces the person wrote and was happy with. Emails they sent, documents they
finished, messages they did not regret. Their sent-items folder is the richest source;
the working folder read in the build's cold open is the second. Ask them to name the
ten; if they cannot, read twenty and watch which they react to.

Not drafts, not things somebody else edited, not templates.

## Step 2, mine

Read all ten and write down what they show, as patterns with the evidence beside each:

- **Openings.** How the first sentence starts. Whether there is a greeting, a
  throat-clear, or a direct start.
- **Closings.** The last line. Sign-off or none.
- **Sentence length and rhythm.** Measured, not guessed. Count a sample.
- **Words that recur** and words that never appear where you would expect them.
- **Structure.** Lists or prose. Headings or none. How transitions happen.
- **Register shifts.** Where they are formal and where they are not, and what triggers
  the change.
- **What is absent.** Adjectives, hedges, exclamation marks, buzzwords. Absence is the
  strongest signal.

Write the first draft of the profile from this, with the ten samples appended at the
bottom. The samples are the original; the description is a lossy copy.

## Step 3, the fifteen gap questions

Samples cannot show what the person would refuse, or why. Draw fifteen questions from
`voice-100q.md`, weighted like this:

- Six from the never list: phrases that make them cringe, things they would never
  write, patterns that mark writing as not theirs.
- Three on beliefs: what they think that others in their field do not.
- Three on register: how they change when serious, when annoyed, when selling.
- Three on structure: how they organise an argument, when they use a list.

One at a time. Push on vagueness. Call out any contradiction with the samples: "your
emails never open with a greeting, and you just said you always do. Which is it?"

## Step 4, show it back

Walk the profile in one line per section. Ask: what did I get wrong? Is there anything
here you do and wish you did not? If you read something drafted from this, what would
make you say "I would never write that"? The last question produces the never list.

## Step 5, let it grow

Every time the person rejects a draft, that rejection is a line for the profile. Say
so on the day. The profile at day thirty should be longer than the profile at day one,
and every added line should be traceable to something they crossed out.

## Frequency guidance, carried into the profile

- HARD RULE: never violate. The never list, and the no-ai-slop standard already in the
  profile template. Strike a slop line only where the samples show the person uses it.
- STRONG TENDENCY: seventy to eighty per cent of the time. Breaking it occasionally is
  fine and looks human.
- LIGHT PREFERENCE: nice to have. Context decides.

Real writers are not consistent. A profile applied mechanically reads as an AI trying
hard to imitate a person. The litmus test on every draft: does this sound like
something they would write, or like an imitation of them?
