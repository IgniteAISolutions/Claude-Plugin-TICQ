# Eval set, draft in my voice

Five requests worth running against a real profile. Run each one twice: once with the
skill on, once with it off, and read the two drafts side by side. The judgement is
subjective and it belongs to the person whose voice it is, so there is no score here.

Run the first one on build day, in the room, before anybody leaves.

## 1. The build-day comparison

*"Here is an email I sent last Tuesday. Draft the same email through my profile without
reading mine first, then show me both."*

Good: the draft opens the way they open, runs at their sentence length, and closes the
way they close. Where it misses, the response names the part of the profile that was
thin and offers a fix to the file.

Bad: a competent business email in the generic house voice, presented as finished, with
no mention of the profile having been read.

## 2. A reply that carries history

*"Draft a reply to this. The client has gone quiet for three weeks and I do not want to
sound like I am chasing."*

Good: asks what came before if the thread is not in front of it, then writes something at
their level of directness rather than a softened house version.

Bad: apologetic filler, a subject line they would never write, or an invented account of what
was agreed earlier.

## 3. A section of a longer document

*"Write the recommendations section of the report for Fernhill. Two pages, they are
sceptical about cost."*

Good: same voice as their email work with the register moved, structure that matches how
they build an argument, and the samples re-read before the closing paragraphs.

Bad: report-language drift, the voice disappearing after the first page, or three
adjectives where the person would use one.

## 4. Rough notes into a client update

*"These are my notes from the call. Turn them into the weekly update for Dan."*

Good: their own phrasing kept wherever it works, editing around it rather than over it,
and a check on anything in the notes that would commit them to something.

Bad: the notes rewritten into consultant prose, or a commitment invented to make the
update read tidily.

## 5. The correction loop

*"That is close but I would never write 'happy to jump on a call'. Fix it."*

Good: the line fixed, plus an offer to write the rule into the voice profile so the next
draft inherits it, naming the file.

Bad: the line fixed silently, leaving the same phrase to reappear next week.
