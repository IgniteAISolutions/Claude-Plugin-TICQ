# Eval set, cowork-meeting-prep

Five requests to run twice: once with the skill on, once with it off. Read both answers
side by side and pick the better one. There is no score and no pass mark. If the
skill-on answer is only as good as the baseline, the skill has not earned its place.

Run these against a real account with real history in it. A clean test account produces
a clean pass and tells you nothing.

---

**1. "Prep me for tomorrow."**

Good: every meeting in the window, with the people named, the last conversation summarised
from a followup note or a thread, the open items with ages, and three things to raise.
Meetings with no history are called out as first conversations.

Bad: a restatement of the calendar with the times and the invitee list, which the person
can already see.

---

**2. "I'm seeing Dan at Westbrook on Thursday, what do I need to know?"**

Good: it finds Dan across email and the working folder, reads the last meeting's
followup note, names what she asked for and what was promised back, and flags the
promise that has gone quiet.

Bad: a general description of Westbrook from one recent email, with no read of what happened
between the two of them last time.

---

**3. "Prep the 10am. There's no recording of the last one."**

Good: it says the recording is missing, prepares from the email thread and the folder
anyway, and never treats the missing recording as evidence the meeting did not happen.

Bad: "there is no record of a previous meeting", or a briefing that quietly starts from
zero.

---

**4. "What's still open with Fernhill before Friday's review?"**

Good: open items only, each with an age and an owner, each traced to a message or a
document. Anything already answered further down the thread is closed off rather than
listed.

Bad: a list built from the newest email in each thread, re-raising things that were
settled three messages later.

---

**5. "Give me the three things worth raising in the Halden call."**

Good: exactly three, each with a reason it matters this week, in the person's own plain
register. Ranked, with the strongest first.

Bad: eleven bullets under a heading saying "possible discussion points", which hands the
choosing back to the person who asked for it to be done.
