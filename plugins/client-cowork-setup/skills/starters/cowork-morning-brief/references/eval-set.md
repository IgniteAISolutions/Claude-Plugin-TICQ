# Eval set, morning brief

Five requests to run against the skill and again with the skill turned off, then compare
the two answers side by side. Plain reading, no scoring. If the skill version is not
obviously the one you would rather receive at 07:00, the skill needs work.

Run these on a real account with a real diary. A clean test folder tells you nothing
about what happens on a Monday.

## 1. "Brief me"

Nothing else said, on an ordinary Tuesday with four meetings and a full inbox.

Good: the six sections in order, one first move that could be done sitting
down in 30 minutes, a time against every line, and every MUST saying whether it is money,
a commitment, or a blocker.

Bad: a tidy summary of the calendar with the inbox and the folder untouched, or a MUST
list of seven items with no times on them.

## 2. "What should I do first?"

Asked at 08:40, with a client call at 09:00.

Good: the first move is preparation for the 09:00 call, or the answer says plainly there
is no useful 30 minute job before it and gives the one thing to check on the way in.

Bad: a first move that takes an hour, or one that depends on somebody else replying.

## 3. A Monday after a long weekend

Three days of mail, two invoices, one client waiting on a document since Friday.

Good: three MUSTs at most, the ranking explained, and a line saying what dropped out of
MUST and why. The unsent Friday draft appears in the waiting section with its age.

Bad: nine MUSTs, or the Friday draft missing entirely because only the inbox was read.

## 4. A quiet day

One meeting, no deadlines, nothing owed to anybody this week.

Good: an empty or one-line MUST section, said plainly, and a first move that uses the
quiet day for something the busy days keep pushing back.

Bad: a SHOULD promoted to MUST to make the brief look busier, or an invented urgency.

## 5. Half the sources are unreachable

Run it with the calendar connector authorised and the working folder disconnected.

Good: the brief still arrives, and it names the folder as NOT CHECKED with what would fix
it. The ranking says out loud that document deadlines were not read.

Bad: a confident brief that reads as complete, with no mention that a third of the input
was missing.
