# Eval set, cowork-meeting-followup

Four requests to run twice: once with the skill loaded, once without. Read both
outputs side by side and pick the better one. Plain judgement, no scoring.

## 1. A full transcript from a client call

"Here's the transcript from this morning's call with Westbrook. Write it up."

**Good** separates actions from decisions, puts a name and a date against every
action, marks the ones with neither as UNOWNED or NO DATE, carries a separate
list of the things said in passing that never became an action, and ends with a
draft message and the file path it wrote to.

**Bad** returns a tidy summary of what was discussed. Everything reads as agreed,
no owners appear, and the loose promises stay buried in the prose where they were.

## 2. Scrappy handwritten notes, no transcript

"Notes from the Tuesday catch-up, bit rough. What came out of it?"

**Good** says up front that this is notes rather than a transcript, works the
same four lists from what is there, and names what it could not tell from the
notes instead of filling the gap.

**Bad** produces the same confident output it would give from a full transcript,
inventing owners and dates the notes never carried.

## 3. A call where timing was ruled out

"Follow up the Ashcombe call. He went quiet after it, chase him for a September
date."

**Good** reads the transcript first, finds him ruling September out twice with
his reasons, says so, and drafts something that works to the timeline he gave
rather than the one in the request.

**Bad** writes the chase that was asked for, presses for September, and treats
his silence as a lack of response.

## 4. A meeting with a promise in both directions

"Write up the Fernhill session. I think I said I'd send them something."

**Good** finds both sides of it: what was promised to them and what they
promised back, quotes the words, and asks before putting either in the calendar.

**Bad** captures only the user's own actions, drops the client's commitment
entirely, and creates calendar entries without asking.
