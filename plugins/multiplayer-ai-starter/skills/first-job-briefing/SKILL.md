# First job: the team briefing

## Purpose

The first job should be one everyone can see, useful from day one, and easy to check. A
short morning briefing, posted to the whole team, is the course's suggested start: it
answers the questions people would otherwise ask each other, and every misstep is in the
open where it can be corrected.

It is also the first sight of what the whole system is for: knowing what is going on and
keeping every person informed. So the briefing always takes the same three-part shape:
**progress** (what moved since yesterday), **actions** (what is owed, by whom, by when),
**next steps** (what happens today and what needs a person's decision).

## Procedure

**1. Start from the repeats.** Read the Repeats section of `multiplayer-pilot.md`. The
questions the team asks most weeks are the first candidates for the briefing.

**2. Draft the specification** with the team:
- Sources: the shared space, the documents and systems the agent can read (from the
  roster's tools column).
- Sections: progress, actions, next steps, in that order. The most-asked repeat goes at
  the top of whichever section it belongs to.
- Length: what a person reads in two minutes.
- Posting time and place: the shared space, never private messages.

**3. Name the reviewer** for the first few days (usually the pilot lead) and agree a
simple score: useful as is, useful with one fix, not useful, with one line of feedback.

**4. Write a rubric for it** with `rubric-builder`, so checking it gets faster each day.

**5. Record** the specification, then each day's score and feedback, in the First job
section of `multiplayer-pilot.md`. Feedback goes into the specification the same day.

## Quality criteria

- The briefing posts to the shared space.
- It reads progress, actions, next steps, and every action names who owes it and by when.
- The most-asked repeat sits at the top of its section.
- A named reviewer scores the first five briefings at least.
- Done means the briefing has run five days with scores recorded and the specification
  updated from the feedback.

## Example

Northgate Supplies (invented). Posted to #ops-orders at 08:30, weekdays.

> **Progress.** Harper: out for delivery today, driver ETA 14:00. Ashby: on schedule for Friday. Price list: winter 2026 is current, dated 14/10/2026.
> **Actions.** Tom: the Lowe pallet label, owed since yesterday; the order is held at the warehouse until it is fixed.
> **Next steps.** Lowe ships once the label is on. Needs a decision from Sam: whether to tell the Lowe customer today or wait until 14:00.
> Reviewer (Priya), day 1: useful with one fix: put the overdue action above progress when something is late.

## Edge cases

- **Nobody reads it.** Shorten it and move the most-asked repeat to the top before
  concluding it failed. Ask one person what they skipped.
- **It gets something wrong.** Correct it in the shared space, visibly, the same morning,
  and add the miss to the rubric.
- **The agent cannot reach a source it needs.** The briefing says so in its own line;
  a silent gap reads as an all clear.
